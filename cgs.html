Castings: Solid results beat expectations, but FY27 outlook cautious

Analysis by Roland Head  ·  Senior Financial Writer

An experienced financial journalist and investor with over a decade covering UK small and mid-cap stocks. I specialise in income and value investing, with a focus on companies generating consistent free cash flow. Learn more

17 Jun 2026 · 12:47pm
4 Comments 389 Reads 11
Analyst's view: Neutral− Unchanged from Sep 2025

Castings has delivered FY26 results ahead of the prior year and ahead of expectations — no small achievement given the headwinds facing the European commercial vehicle sector throughout the period. The business remains cash-generative, net cash, and the final dividend of 18.5p is maintained. However, management's cautious tone on FY27 — reflecting uncertainty in the commercial vehicle cycle — keeps me at NEUTRAL for now. The shares look inexpensive at sub-10x earnings with net cash and a 50-year dividend record, but I want more confidence in the earnings trajectory before turning positive.

Background

Castings (CGS) is a West Midlands-based manufacturer of iron castings for the commercial vehicle, agricultural and general industrial markets. The company has operated from its Brownhills site for over 100 years and is one of the last independent UK foundries operating at meaningful scale.

Castings is notable for its capital discipline and conservative management culture. It has paid an unbroken dividend for 50 years, maintains a net cash position, and has never taken on significant debt. Management runs the business for long-term sustainability rather than short-term earnings maximisation.

FY26 final results

Results for the year ended 31 March 2026, showing resilience in difficult market conditions:

  • Revenue and profit ahead of prior year despite softer European commercial vehicle market
  • Iron casting volumes resilient — Castings benefited from UK-focused customer mix
  • Net cash position maintained; balance sheet remains ungeared
  • Final dividend maintained at 18.5p per share
  • Management cautious on FY27 outlook given commercial vehicle cycle uncertainty
  • Long-term electrification risk for iron casting demand acknowledged but seen as slow-moving

The company noted that while the European truck market remains soft, UK-based customers have been more resilient. The FY27 guidance tone is cautious but not alarming, with management expecting "broadly similar" conditions in the near term.

Financial review

Castings operates a simple, predictable financial model: manufacture components, sell them to OEMs, collect cash, pay a dividend. There is no financial engineering, no acquisitions, no complex accounting. This simplicity makes the business easier to analyse and harder to get wrong.

The net cash position is a genuine strength at this stage of the commercial vehicle cycle. Competitors with debt face pressure during downturns; Castings has the balance sheet to sustain the dividend and maintain capital investment even in weak periods.

The long-term EV question is the bear case. Iron castings are less suited to electric powertrains, which have fewer and lighter metal components. However, the commercial vehicle transition to EV is progressing far more slowly than the passenger car transition, partly due to range requirements and partly due to fleet economics. Management estimates the meaningful impact is at least 5–7 years away, during which time the shares offer a very attractive risk/return for patient investors.
