Games Workshop: 90p interim dividend and another period of strong delivery

Analysis by Graham Neary, CFA  ·  Senior Financial Writer

Analyst's view: Positive− Unchanged from Mar 2026

Games Workshop continues to reward patient shareholders. The 90p interim dividend — up 12.5% on the prior period — is the latest in a long run of dividend increases, and reflects the board's confidence in the durability of the Warhammer franchise. H1 revenue of £352m and operating profit of £162m are both ahead of our estimates, with the royalty income from licensing (including the Amazon TV deal) becoming a meaningful contributor at £42m. I'm maintaining POSITIVE — this remains one of the most durable franchise businesses in UK mid and large caps.

Background

Games Workshop (GAW) is the creator of the Warhammer tabletop miniature wargaming universe, with two core product lines — Warhammer 40,000 (science fiction) and Age of Sigmar (fantasy). The company designs, manufactures and sells miniature models, rulebooks and hobby accessories globally through its own stores, independent retailers and an online platform.

Games Workshop is unusual in the consumer sector for its combination of: a loyal, deeply engaged customer base; extremely high operating margins (typically 35–40%); a growing IP licensing business; and a management philosophy that prioritises long-term franchise health over short-term revenue maximisation.

H1 results summary

Today's announcement declares the interim dividend and provides an H1 trading summary. Key figures for the six months ended 1 December 2025:

  • Revenue £352m, ahead of prior year and analyst estimates
  • Operating profit £162m at an operating margin of approximately 46%
  • Net cash £198m at the half-year end
  • Royalty income £42m, driven by the Amazon Warhammer series and other licensing deals
  • Interim dividend 90p per share (prior period: 80p per share)
  • Dividend payable 30 June 2026 to shareholders on register 12 June 2026

The Amazon Warhammer series has been renewed for a second season, providing an additional royalty stream beyond the initial deal. Management noted continued strong sell-through of core Warhammer 40,000 product, with Squat (Leagues of Votann) and Space Marine 2 video game tie-in products exceeding expectations.

Financial review

Games Workshop's economics are exceptional by any measure. An operating margin of 46% in a manufacturing business reflects the pricing power that comes from owning the world's dominant tabletop wargaming IP. The cost of goods sold is a fraction of retail price — collectors pay a premium for the hobby experience, not just the physical product.

The royalty income line is increasingly important. At £42m per half-year run rate, it is equivalent to approximately 6% of total revenue but requires virtually no incremental capital. If the Amazon series follows the trajectory of other major fantasy franchises, this stream could grow substantially.

The balance sheet is pristine: net cash of £198m with no debt. This funds the dividend policy, which has paid out approximately 90% of post-tax profits in recent years. The reinvestment requirement is low — moulds, IP development and modest retail estate — leaving substantial surplus cash for shareholders.
